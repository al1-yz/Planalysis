# Domain rules

Planalysis conventions for financial behavior. These are deterministic rules of this
application — not claims about how any bank behaves. Each rule must be covered by unit tests
when implemented.

## Money

- All BRL amounts are integer cents (`MoneyCents`, a lightweight branded `number`).
  `R$ 10,50` → `1050`. Values must be safe integers, and so must the result of every addition
  or subtraction; an out-of-range result is an error, never a silently imprecise number. Zero and
  negative amounts (e.g. refunds) are valid.
- Formatted strings are for display only, using
  `Intl.NumberFormat("pt-BR", { style: "currency", currency: "BRL" })`. Formatting lives in the UI
  layer (`src/features`, `src/components`), never in `src/domain`.
- Arithmetic stays in integers.
- Percentage calculations are not implemented yet. When a feature needs them, the convention is
  that percentages producing fractional cents are rounded half away from zero.

## Installment allocation

`sum(installments) === total`, always.

Rule: divide the total by the number of installments (integer division). The remainder cents
are added one each to the **first** installments.

Example: R$ 100,00 in 3 → R$ 33,34 · R$ 33,33 · R$ 33,33.

A negative total (refund) is split the same way in magnitude: -R$ 100,00 in 3 → -R$ 33,34 ·
-R$ 33,33 · -R$ 33,33. A total smaller than the count gives one cent each to the first
installments and zero to the rest. The count must be a positive integer.

There is no maximum count yet. One must be set before installment counts become user-controlled.

## Dates

| Concept                          | Representation                                  |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Calendar date (transaction etc.) | `LocalDate` string `YYYY-MM-DD`, no time/zone   |
| Accounting period                | `YearMonth` string `YYYY-MM`                    |
| Record timestamps                | ISO-8601 UTC string (`createdAt`, `updatedAt`)  |
| Day-of-month settings            | integer 1–31 (closing day, due day, recurrence) |

- `LocalDate` is never parsed with `new Date("YYYY-MM-DD")` (that is UTC and can shift the day).
- Pure date calculations (validation, clamping, month arithmetic, invoice assignment) are calendar
  arithmetic and never depend on the device time zone.
- Years are four digits, `0001` to `9999`. Invalid values (`2026-02-30`, month `13`, non-padded
  strings) are rejected with a `RangeError`, and so is any operation whose result falls outside
  those years (e.g. `addMonths` past `9999-12`, or an invoice after `9999-12`).
- "Today" is taken from the device's local calendar date. This is the only device input; it is
  passed into the use cases that need it (never read inside the domain) and is not implemented
  yet. It will be added with the first feature that needs it.
- A day-of-month that does not exist in a given month is clamped to that month's last day
  (closing day 31 → 30 April, 28 or 29 February).

## Identifiers and timestamps

- Every record has a UUID (lowercase, canonical form) generated by the application, never by the
  database.
- `createdAt` and `updatedAt` are set by the application; `createdAt` never changes after the
  record is created.

## Accounts, cards and categories

- An account has an **opening balance** and an **opening date**: the balance at the start of that
  day, before that day's transactions. The opening date defaults to the day the account is created
  and may be moved earlier (to enter history), never later than its earliest transaction.
- An account's balance on a date is its opening balance plus its transactions up to that date. It
  is always computed, never stored.
- A credit card is not an account. It has a closing day and a due day (1–31) and an optional
  credit limit. Purchases on it are assigned to invoices by the rule below.
- A category is either income or expense.
- Names are unique among active records of the same kind (categories: within the same
  income/expense kind), ignoring case and surrounding or repeated spaces. Accents are
  significant. The name of an archived record can be reused.
- **Archiving** hides an account, card or category from new use but keeps its history; no new
  transaction may refer to an archived record, but existing ones stay editable. A record that
  anything refers to cannot be deleted, only archived.

## Transactions

- A transaction has a signed amount: positive is money in, negative is money out. It is never
  zero.
- It belongs to exactly one account or one card, and has a date: when the financial event
  happened. There is no pending or settled state; a recorded transaction has happened.
- A transaction cannot be dated before its account's opening date, and cannot be dated after
  today.
- It may have a category. An explicit category decides whether it counts as income or expense;
  without one, its sign does. A refund is a positive amount in an expense category: it reduces
  that category's spending instead of counting as income.
- A payment to a person or institution outside the user's accounts is an ordinary transaction
  with a description, not a transfer.
- Transactions can be edited and deleted; there is no audit trail.

## Transfers

A transfer between two owned accounts is never counted as income or expense. It only moves
balance between accounts.

It is stored as two linked transactions (the legs): a negative one on the source account and a
positive one on the destination account, with equal and opposite amounts, the same date, no
category, and a shared transfer id. The two legs are always created, changed and deleted together,
and a leg cannot be changed on its own. Editing an existing transfer is allowed even if one of its
accounts has since been archived, as long as its accounts are unchanged; new transfers may not use
an archived account.

## Credit card invoice assignment — Planalysis convention

Given a card's closing day (clamped per month as above):

| Purchase date           | Invoice         |
| ----------------------- | --------------- |
| before the closing date | current invoice |
| **on** the closing date | next invoice    |
| after the closing date  | next invoice    |

The invoice is identified by the `YearMonth` of its closing date: with closing day 10, a
purchase on 2026-03-05 is on invoice `2026-03` and one on 2026-03-10 is on `2026-04`.

This is a fixed Planalysis convention, not a universal banking rule. It may become configurable
per card in the future; it is not configurable now.
